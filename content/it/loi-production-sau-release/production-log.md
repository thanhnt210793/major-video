## Viewer questions and misunderstandings

## Lessons learned
- The reviewer's feedback each round pushed toward felt pressure, realism and an inner monologue. Write the investigation as a misdirection arc (deny → doubt → suspect others → it's me) from the first draft next time.
- Session scratchpad files can disappear between sessions. Keep anything a later step depends on (chosen voice takes, generators) inside the repo.
- A narration-only mix with quiet SFX reads as "chán" to the reviewer. Plan a music bed and strong accent SFX from the first build of a dramatic story.
- Picking the "most expressive" TTS take by pitch movement alone can pick a garbled take (hook take 3 was unintelligible). Always run tools/check-takes.mjs (Whisper) before using a high-temperature take.
- Whisper large-v3-turbo hallucinates "Hãy subscribe cho kênh Ghiền Mì Gõ…" on some short Vietnamese clips (the Thái Sơn voice, one hook take). Treat that text as "unusable timing", not as the audio content.
- Inserting a scene shifts every frame number after it. Tools that hard-coded frame numbers (SFX cue sheet, music segments, voice plan) had to be rewritten, and one crashed at build. Key per-video tools by scene id from the start.
- HyperFrames caption grouping is syllable-based and splits Vietnamese compound words. Phrase the captions by hand per scene (tools/caption-phrases.mjs here) on every video; it is worth moving into scripts/ for the channel.
- Reviewer feedback in casual Vietnamese can be ambiguous about who speaks ("sếp nên nói là cho em…" fits both the boss and the developer). Offer each reading as an option with a preview instead of guessing.
- A real-life process the story skips over (here: the half hour of searching before the reveal) is what a technical reviewer misses first. When a story jumps from a problem straight to its answer, ask whether to show the search.
